 UptoZishu: the Android app store for indie developers (website and native app)

Updated Nov 23, 2026

UptoZishu is a free Android app store built for independent developers and for people who like to discover apps beyond the big stores. It has two parts that work together: a website where anyone can browse and read about apps, and a native Android app that downloads them, keeps them up to date and tells you when something new arrives.

Download the Android app (version 1.0.0): uptozishu_v1.0.0.apk

Website: uptozishu.zishuai.cloud

Why UptoZishu exists

Small developers often build good apps that nobody sees, because large stores are crowded and rankings favour big names. On the other side, people who download apps from random APK sites have no way to know who made the app or whether anyone looked at it first.

UptoZishu tries to sit between the two. Developers publish for free from their own console. Every submission is checked by our team before it appears. Each app has an information page with its developer, permissions and reviews, so you can decide before you install.

This article walks through everything: the name change, the website, the native app, how to install it, the Coming soon system, how trust works, what developers can do, how updates and notifications work, and how paid placements are labelled.

Stardust Store is now UptoZishu

This project was first called Stardust Store. The name has been changed to UptoZishu. It is the same store, the same team and the same apps. Only the name and the look of the brand changed.

What the change means for you:

  • Website: the store lives at uptozishu.zishuai.cloud and the developer console at dev.uptozishu.zishuai.cloud. Everything on the site now says UptoZishu.
  • Android app: the native app is called UptoZishu and its package name is com.uptozishu. If you used a beta or test build under the old name, install the new app from the link in this article.
  • Developers: your account, apps and developer profile stay as they were. You publish and manage them from the UptoZishu Developer Console.
  • Old name in some places: the project's code repository and the download link still contain the words "Stardust-store", because that is the name of the repository on GitHub. This is only a technical address, not a different product.

If you see "Stardust Store" in an old post or a screenshot, it refers to this same project.

The website

The website at uptozishu.zishuai.cloud is the public face of the store. You do not need an account to browse it.

How it is built

Every app page, category page and list page is generated ahead of time as a plain static page from the store's database. The pages are rebuilt automatically about every 10 minutes, so a newly approved app, an edited description or a removed app shows up within a short time without anyone editing the site by hand. Static pages load quickly even on a slow connection, and search engines can read them easily. A sitemap is generated in the same build, so new pages are listed for search engines automatically.

What you can do on it

  • Home: shelves such as Sponsored, Featured slides, Editor's choice and a Coming soon shelf, so you can see what is new and what is on the way.
  • Browse by category: each category has its own page with its apps. Popular apps, Popular games and Top lists are available too.
  • Search: search by app name and open results directly. Upcoming apps are included in search and clearly marked.
  • App pages: every app has a page with its icon, screenshots, description, version, size, download button, ratings and reviews.
  • App information card: one card holds the details that matter before you install: the developer, developer verification, feedback, the permissions the app asks for, and security information. Tapping verification, permissions or security opens a bottom sheet with the full explanation, the same way the native app does. Permissions are shown as small labelled pills, and a green tick appears when a developer is verified.
  • Developer pages: each developer has a public page listing their apps.
  • Reviews and ratings: signed-in users can rate and review apps.
  • Report an app: if something looks wrong, you can send a report to our team. You receive a confirmation email for your report.
  • Library: keep a list of apps you saved so you can find them again.
  • Coming soon page: all upcoming apps are collected on one page, each with a Pre-register button.
  • About, Support, Privacy and Terms: plain pages that explain who runs the store, how to get help and how your data and paid placements are handled.

What the website does not do

The website does not install apps by itself. The download button gives you the APK file, and Android asks you to confirm the install. The native app makes this smoother, as the next section explains.

The native Android app

The UptoZishu app is a native Android app written in Java. It is not a website wrapped in an app shell: the screens, lists and animations are drawn by the app itself. It uses the same catalog as the website, so what you see on the site is what you see in the app.

Discover

  • Home screen with shelves for Sponsored, Featured, Editor's choice and Coming soon apps, plus categories.
  • Search with filters. Narrow results by category, rating or size, and tap one of your recent searches to repeat it.
  • App pages with screenshots, description, version, size, ratings and reviews.
  • Reviews you can sort. Mark a review as helpful, and sort reviews to bring the most useful ones first.
  • Saved apps. Save an app to your library to find it again later.

Download and install

  • The app has its own downloader. You see progress, and you can pause a download and resume it later. If the connection drops, the download continues from where it stopped instead of starting over.
  • When the file is ready, the app hands it to Android's installer. Android always asks you to confirm an installation, and no app can skip that step.
  • The app shows which of your apps are installed and which version you have, so it can tell you when a newer version exists.

Stay up to date

  • A background check looks for new versions of the apps you installed and sends one notification when updates are available. Tap it to open the Updates screen.
  • Update all downloads the updates one after another, two at a time, and asks you to confirm each install.
  • The store can update itself. When a new version of UptoZishu is published in the store, the app shows a banner, and the update is installed last because installing it restarts the app.
  • You can switch update notifications on or off in your profile.

Know what you install

  • Each app page has an information section with developer verification, permissions and security details, opened as a bottom sheet. Permissions appear as pills, one per permission.
  • Apps that are not released yet are shown as Coming soon, and you can pre-register for them (see the Coming soon section).

Account

You can sign in to review apps, mark reviews helpful and pre-register. Your profile screen shows your saved apps, your pending updates and your settings.

Download and install the app

The current version is 1.0.0. The official download is the APK file published in the project's GitHub release:

https://github.com/zishutron/Stardust-store/releases/download/v1.0.0.0/uptozishu_v1.0.0.apk

Install it in these steps:

  1. Open the link above on your Android phone and download the file uptozishu_v1.0.0.apk.
  2. Open the downloaded file. Android will say that apps from this source cannot be installed. Tap Settings and allow your browser or file manager to install unknown apps.
  3. Go back and tap Install. If Google Play Protect shows a warning, it is because the app does not come from the Play Store. Read the message and continue only if you downloaded the file from the official link above.
  4. Open UptoZishu. On Android 13 and newer, allow notifications when asked, so the app can tell you about updates and releases.
  5. Browse, search and install apps. Sign in when you want to review an app or pre-register for an upcoming one.

After this first install you do not need the link again. When a new version of UptoZishu is published, the app shows a banner and installs the update from inside the store.

Good to know

  • Always download the APK only from the official link in this article or from the website, never from a third-party mirror.
  • Android asks for confirmation every time an app is installed or updated. This is an Android safety rule, and it applies to every app store outside the Play Store.
  • If an install fails, check that you have enough free storage and that the downloaded file is complete, then try again.

Coming soon and pre-register

A developer can list an app before it is ready, the same way a pre-release works in other stores. The app gets a Coming soon page, and people can pre-register to be told when it is released.

How it works from start to finish:

  1. The developer chooses Pre-release when submitting an app in the Developer Console.
  2. Our team reviews the submission. When it is approved, the developer gets an email and the app appears as Coming soon.
  3. The app shows on the home screen's Coming soon shelf, on the Coming soon page of the website, in its category and in search, each time marked as Coming soon. It has no download button and no reviews yet.
  4. Signed-in users tap Pre-register. It is free, and the app page shows how many people have pre-registered.
  5. When the app is ready, the developer taps Release now. The release goes through a quick review by our team, then the app becomes a normal live app with a download button.
  6. People who pre-registered get a notification in the UptoZishu Android app saying the app is now available. Tapping it opens the app page.

What we do and do not send

  • Pre-registering does not send you any email, and you will not get an email when the app is released. We do not store your email address for this.
  • The release notification is shown only inside the native Android app, and only to people who use it.
  • The app is not downloaded automatically. Android requires you to confirm every installation, so the notification takes you to the app page and you tap Download.

When the notification arrives

The Android app checks the catalog when you open it and also in the background about every six hours. If an app you pre-registered for has been released, it notifies you then. The website rebuilds about every 10 minutes, so there can be a short delay after the developer releases. If you pre-registered on the website, open the Android app once before the release so it knows about your registration.

Trust and safety

An app store is only useful if you can trust what is in it. UptoZishu uses several layers, and it is honest about what each one does and does not promise.

  • Review before publishing. Every new app and every update is checked by our team in the admin panel before it goes live. Submissions can be approved, or sent back with a reason so the developer can fix them.
  • Verified badge. A Verified badge means our team reviewed the developer's profile. It is not a guarantee or an endorsement of any app. The app page shows the badge with a green tick, and tapping it explains what it means.
  • Permissions in plain view. The permissions an app asks for are listed on its page as pills, so you can see them before you install.
  • Reviews and helpful votes. Real users can rate and review an app, and mark other reviews as helpful.
  • Reports. Anyone can report an app. Our team reads the report, and you get a confirmation email so you know it arrived.
  • Suspension. If a developer breaks the rules, their account can be suspended and their apps taken down.

What we cannot promise

Review by a small team lowers risk but cannot remove it. Treat any app the way you would treat any download: check the developer, read the reviews, look at the permissions, and install only what you need.

For developers

Publishing on UptoZishu is free. Developers use the UptoZishu Developer Console to upload and manage everything.

What the console offers

  • Upload an app. Add the APK, icon, screenshots, category, description and other details. You can choose to Submit it for review, save it as a Draft and finish later, or list it as a Pre-release (Coming soon).
  • Manage your apps. Edit details, upload new versions, and see the status of each app: live, pending, draft, rejected, suspended or Coming soon.
  • Updates go through review too. When you upload a new version, our team checks it before it reaches users.
  • Release a pre-release. When a Coming soon app is ready, tap Release now to send it for approval and make it live.
  • Analytics. See how your apps are doing.
  • Support and notifications. Open a support ticket and get replies in the console and by email. Decisions on your apps and plans also arrive as notifications.
  • Developer profile. Build a public profile that appears on your store developer page, and apply for the Verified badge.

Emails developers receive

Developers get an email when a ticket is opened and answered, when an app or update is approved or needs changes, when a pre-release is listed as Coming soon, and when a paid plan is approved or not approved.

Optional paid placements

Paying is never needed to publish. Developers who want more visibility can buy a plan for 1, 2, 3, 6 or 12 months. The payment is made by UPI, and our team checks the payment details and the request before the plan starts. After payment, a plan usually goes live within 3 to 5 days. If something is wrong, the request is rejected and the developer is told why.

How the Android app checks

  • When you open the app, or come back to it, it refreshes the catalog (at most once a minute), so released, edited and removed apps are picked up.
  • In the background, Android runs a check about every six hours when a connection is available. It continues after the phone restarts.
  • Each new version is announced once. Several updates are grouped into one notification.
  • You can turn update notifications off in your profile.

Limits you should know

  • Background checks are timed by Android. Battery saver or aggressive background limits on some phones can delay them.
  • The store does not use push messages, so a notification can arrive later than the moment an app is published. The website also needs up to about 10 minutes to rebuild after a change.
  • Users of the website and store accounts are not sent marketing email.

Privacy and how money works

Who pays and who does not

  • Users never pay. Browsing, downloading and reviewing are free.
  • Developers pay only for optional extras such as the Verified badge, Sponsored and Featured placements.
  • Paid placements are labelled. Apps in the Sponsored or Featured lists, and developers with a Verified badge, may have paid for that place. These lists mix paid and hand-picked apps, and they are not a ranking of quality.
  • Paying never buys access to your data. A developer who pays for a placement gets no access to users' personal information.

Your data

  • We keep what is needed to run the store: the account details you give when you sign in, your reviews and helpful votes, the reports you send and the apps you pre-registered for.
  • A pre-registration is stored against your account with the time you made it. It is not used to send you email.
  • Saved apps, recent searches and notification choices in the Android app are kept on your phone.
  • The website has its own Privacy and Terms pages that explain this in full, and the developer console has its own terms for developers.

If you want an account or a review removed, use the Support page or contact us through the channels listed there.

Frequently asked questions

Is UptoZishu the same as Stardust Store? Yes. Stardust Store was the earlier name. The project is now called UptoZishu.

Is it free? Yes, for users. Developers publish for free, and only optional promotion plans cost money.

Do I need the website and the app? No. The website works on its own for browsing and downloading. The Android app adds smoother downloads, update notifications, Update all and release alerts.

Why does Android ask me to allow unknown apps? Because UptoZishu is installed outside the Play Store. Android asks the same of every app installed this way.

Will apps install automatically? No. Android requires you to confirm each install, so the store cannot install or update an app silently.

How do I get notified about an upcoming app? Sign in, tap Pre-register on its Coming soon page, and keep notifications allowed in the Android app.

Will I get an email when a pre-registered app is released? No. The alert is a notification in the Android app only.

How do updates work? The Android app checks for new versions of your installed apps, tells you once, and lets you update one by one or all together.

How can I tell an app was checked? Every app is reviewed before it goes live. Look for the Verified badge on the developer, read the permissions list and the reviews.

An app looks suspicious. What do I do? Use the report option on the app page. Our team reviews it.

I am a developer. How do I start? Open the Developer Console, create your account, and upload your app. Choose Submit, Draft or Pre-release.

Does paying get my app approved? No. Paid plans only affect where an approved app is shown, and every app and update is still reviewed.
